# A shortfall characterization is a bisected per-game localization

ADR-0030's amendment set the bar a tracking-shortfall pin must clear — "a characterized outage, never 'the derive said so'" — and ADR-0058's amendment made pins move only by PR, with the season loop's stuck-frontier alarm as the system's way of asking a human for one. Neither ADR said what a characterization **is**. This one does: a pin is earned by **localizing the season gap to specific games with bisected `DateTo`-anchored tracking pulls**, and by nothing weaker. The tool is `ingestion/localize_shortfall.py`; the standing definition lands in CONTEXT.md's Tracking shortfall entry and `season.config.json`'s `_readme`.

**The mechanism.** The tracking dashboards are season-aggregate — no GAME_ID exists anywhere in a `playerdashptshots` response — so per-game attribution cannot be read off any snapshot. But the endpoint respects `DateTo` (the ADR-0058 spike's finding, the same mechanics the coherence check runs on), so the cumulative shortfall through date X — official pre-drop FGA through X minus the tracking General family's Σ FGA through X — is a **non-decreasing step function over the hero's game dates**. Every step is one outage game; bisection finds each in ~log2(games) pulls. The 2026-08-10 characterizations ran 7 pulls over Sarr's 48 games, 8 over Carrington's 82, and 7 over Reaves's 51, against 48 + 82 + 51 for the linear alternative. Official cumulative FGA comes from the committed raw shot snapshot's per-shot rows; the target is the pre-drop total, the same denominator the General identity is stated against.

**The fingerprint that confirms an outage.** At each localized game, the per-family delta across the anchored snapshot pair brackets the game exactly: every tracking family (General, Shot Clock, Closest Defender, Dribble, Touch Time) short by the **same** count is a tracking-side gap in the optical record — a missing segment hits all derived views identically. A family-specific discrepancy would be corruption instead (a family disagreeing with its own universe hard-fails, ADR-0030), and a negative step is contradiction, never outage. All three 2026-08-10 games carried the clean fingerprint: Carrington's `0022500029` (2025-11-07), every family short by exactly 1 of his 12 attempts; Sarr's `0022500938` (2026-03-10), every family short by exactly 6 of his 19; Reaves's `0022500906` (2026-03-05), every family short by exactly 1 of his 11.

**Why circumstance is banned: the method falsified it on first contact.** The day before the bisection existed, Carrington's 1-attempt shortfall was pinned to `0022500900` — the 2026-03-05 league-wide meltdown night already characterized for Ace Bailey — on the reasoning that it was the only characterized-outage game he had played. Plausible, evidence-adjacent, and **wrong**: the bisection put his missing attempt on 2025-11-07, a night no existing pin covered (and it also surfaced that he had played the newly discovered Sarr outage game, so the "only candidate" premise was false twice over). A circumstantial pin fails silently in the worst way — the guard's season-sum assertion passes on a wrong per-game attribution, and the error only detonates later, at a mid-season frontier where the coherence check needs the per-game grain to be *true* (a wrong-date pin explains a gap it shouldn't or misses one it should, and the loop retreats or halts on clean data). **Proximity to a characterized outage is not characterization.**

**Amendment (2026-08-11) — the rule survives proximity being right.** The tenth hero add supplied the other half of the test. Austin Reaves's 1-attempt 2025-26 shortfall bisected in 7 pulls to `0022500906` on **2026-03-05** — the same night as Ace Bailey's already-characterized `0022500900`. Proximity, in other words, would have pointed at the right night. The rule does not soften, for two reasons, and the second is the one to remember.

First, the localization is what established the date; the match to Ace's pin arrived afterward and could only ever be corroboration. Before the bisection, "2026-03-05 was a bad night league-wide" was exactly as available a story for Carrington, and for him it was false. The same prior, applied to two heroes on the same roster in the same session, produced one right answer and one wrong one. A prior with that record carries no information, and no contributor can tell from the inside which case they are in.

Second, and more precisely: **the gap is per player-game, not per game and certainly not per night.** Read the three heroes who were on the floor on 2026-03-05 together. Reaves's pin is `0022500906`. Ace's is `0022500900`. And Carrington played `0022500900` too — the same game as Ace, Wizards at Utah — with **no gap in it at all**, his single missing attempt sitting four months earlier on 2025-11-07. Two players in one game, one short five attempts and the other short none, is exactly what a partial outage looks like from the inside: a missing segment of the optical record costs a player only the attempts he happened to take inside it. Ace shot during the dark stretch; Carrington did not.

That is what makes per-night reasoning unsalvageable rather than merely unreliable. Knowing the night, and even knowing the game, still does not tell you whose attempts went missing or how many. Since the coherence check consumes the per-game grain (ADR-0058), a pin that is right about the night and wrong about the game — or right about both and wrong about the count — fails in precisely the silent way the ban exists to prevent. Proximity remains, as before, not characterization, including when it happens to agree.

**Mechanics that must hold.** Investigation snapshots land in `tracking/investigation/`, a subdirectory on purpose: `derive_creation` treats the lexicographically last `*.json` in the flat `tracking/` dir as the latest real snapshot, so a stamped investigation file there would silently become every later derive's input. Existing investigation snapshots are reused instead of re-pulled — append-only holds (ADR-0006), reruns are free, and a teammate's overlapping night costs nothing. The tool prints the pin candidate and **never writes the config**: the pin still travels by PR with its evidence recorded in the `_readme` (ADR-0058), and the localization is the evidence. Pulls are local-only like every pull.

**Consequences:** the stuck-frontier alarm now has a standing answer — run the localization, review the fingerprint, pin by PR — instead of an unwritten investigation each time. A future contributor must not pin a shortfall by co-occurrence with a known outage night, must not accept a localization whose steps fail to sum to the season shortfall, must not land investigation snapshots in the flat `tracking/` directory, and must not teach the season loop to run this tool — the loop asks for a characterization by halting; producing one stays human-initiated (ADR-0058's boundary).
