# Every game is priced against the league as it stood entering its day

A game's selection and making values are computed against its **pre-game ruler**: the league's `LeagueAverages` zone frame through the day before the game, inclusive, pulled once and never changed. Every game on a date shares one ruler. When no league game has been played before the date (a team playing on the league's opening night), the ruler would be empty, and the game is priced against the **prior season's full baseline** instead, labeled as such. Season-to-date views (the season profile, a player's season zones) keep the frontier ruler, labeled as the season's, as hero pages do today. Anything plotted per game, including the quadrant and the ledger's decomposition, uses each game's own ruler, so a dot always matches its card.

**Why not the frontier ruler for cards.** On game night the frontier's league frame can include games still in progress, the card would change every morning after it was shared, and ranks across a season would mix rulers without saying so. **Why not last season's baseline all year.** It compares this season's shots to last season's shooting, against the reasoning of ADR-0061 that each season is measured against its own league. The pre-game ruler is complete before tip-off, identical for every game on a date, reproducible from the raw layer, and stable forever, which is what makes game files immutable (ADR-0088) and what makes two seasons' games comparable on one chart: each dot is priced against its own league as it stood that day.

**The fallback is for an empty ruler only, set from data.** The plan proposed borrowing last season's ruler for any thin early ruler and asked the measurement to set the bar (`docs/research/pre-game-ruler-2025-26.md`). On the 2025-26 Jazz, the first game's ruler held two league games (356 attempts, 20 in the thinnest zone) and still moved selection 0.015 and making 0.011 against the season-end ruler, inside the verdict ladder's 0.02 neutral band; from the fourth game nothing moved more than 0.009. A thin ruler is accurate enough, so the fallback is kept only where there is nothing to price against. Decided 2026-09-28.

**How a ruler is pulled and stored.** `live_pulls.pull_ruler_snapshot` pulls the frame with `DateTo` only, through a near-shotless player: the frame does not depend on the pull's subject (UTA and OKC pulls through one date returned identical frames), and it does respect `DateFrom`, so a ruler is never windowed. Rulers land under `data/raw/_league/<season>/rulers/<through>/` and load into the record store's `league_ruler_zone` at the frame's own fine grain, keyed by through-date (`ingestion/load_rulers.py`). A ruler describes a past date, so it never grows: a re-pull that disagrees is a correction and halts. An empty frame is a valid observation and records its head, so "no games yet" is distinguishable from "never pulled".

**Every card states its ruler's game count.** The frame states league attempts, never how many games they came from, so the count comes from the league game log (`leaguegamelog`, team grain, pulled beside the rulers into `league_game`): the games on or before the through-date. A game log that has games but a ruler with no attempts, or the reverse, is a contradiction and halts the export. The log stores each game's two teams in id order, never as home and visitor, because its `MATCHUP` reads "@" for both rows of a neutral-site game (five in 2025-26, the Mexico City game among them); home comes from the box score.

**Consequences.** A ruler costs one small call per game day and the game log one per pull session; both are reproducible later from any date, so a missed night backfills. The game payload's `_meta.ruler` names the kind, the season, the through-date, the game count, and the source snapshot, and the card says it in one structural line. A future contributor must not price a card against the frontier ruler, must not reintroduce a thin-ruler fallback without a new measurement, and must not compute a game count from anything but the league's own record of played games.
